MARKSMANSHIP HUNTER ROTATION IN MIDNIGHT SEASON 2
The rotation for Marksmanship Hunter focuses on maximizing your use of certain buffs, and maximizing your number of uses of cooldown-limited abilities. Marksmanship has a fairly consistent flow, where one ability logically flows into the next, with a few priority shifts based on procs and buff uptimes.
	 Precise Shots: These procs are guaranteed to happen from all  Aimed Shots, and, depending on Talents,  Rapid Fire as well. They are spent on  Arcane Shot on single-target and  Multi-Shot on 2+ targets, at least when we play  Trick Shots. Every single proc must be spent, which in practice means following up every  Aimed Shot and every  Rapid Fire with an  Arcane Shot or  Multi-Shot.
	Always Be Casting: Casting as many abilities as possible matters more than pressing the perfect button every time, and dead time is the single biggest thing holding most players back from their DPS goals.  Steady Shot is how you fill it, and it is castable while moving, so there is no excuse for a dead global. The best Marksmanship Hunters will have way more  Steady Shot casts than someone more average, in large part because they do not ever stop casting when moving or dealing with mechanics.
	Movement: While not strictly a rotational concept, while the flow of Marksmanship is straightforward, managing your movement is a big part of mastering it. The core thing to remember is that you should try and reserve movement for when you are not casting  Aimed Shot. Whenever you are not casting  Aimed Shot, you can move freely without interrupting your rotation, and you should take advantage of this fact to always think "can I be positioned ever so slightly better" and to move accordingly. Standing still while not casting  Aimed Shot is rarely the best move.

BEST MARKSMANSHIP HUNTER SINGLE TARGET ROTATION
Below is the priority system that describes the optimal single-target rotation for Marksmanship Hunter.

The core loop of Marksmanship is to cast  Aimed Shot and  Rapid Fire on cooldown. These will both activate  Precise Shots, which buffs  Arcane Shot/ Kill Shot/ Black Arrow ( Dark Ranger) significantly. Every  Aimed Shot and every  Rapid Fire should thus be followed up by a "PS Spender" like this. So,  Aimed Shot on cooldown,  Rapid Fire on cooldown, and a PS Spender after each of them. On top of this, we add  Explosive Shot and  Volley on cooldown.  Steady Shot is your filler when you can do absolutely nothing else.

MARKSMANSHIP HUNTER MAJOR COOLDOWNS
Marksmanship only has one cooldown of note in  Trueshot. Most of its value is in its crit chance and crit damage buff, and it also speeds up our  Aimed Shot and  Rapid Fire cooldowns. It changes little about how we play, but it speeds things up considerably.
Trueshot
TRUESHOT
 Trueshot massively increases our critical strike chance, damage, and the cooldown rates of  Aimed Shot and  Rapid Fire, as well as  Aimed Shot's cast time. It makes us do everything stronger and faster.

Our rotational priorities barely change inside  Trueshot. The handful that do are in the priority lists above.

For  Dark Ranger, activating Trueshot also gives us a  Black Arrow proc, which we need to activate  Precise Shots before we can fire.

For both Hero Talents, activating  Trueshot turns the Trueshot button into another ability:  Moonlight Chakram for  Sentinel, and  Wailing Arrow for  Dark Ranger. Each one is a single cast, so use it where it will not push back your other priorities. For  Sentinel on single target that means firing  Moonlight Chakram with roughly 5 seconds of  Trueshot left.
MARKSMANSHIP HUNTER ADVANCED TIPS
You could argue that it is a basic tip, but it is still the #1 issue that even "good" Marksmanship Hunters face, and it is to ensure that you are always casting something. Marksmanship always has something to cast, because even if you run out of absolutely every resource and cooldown, you can always  Steady Shot, and within 2 seconds, you are back to casting  Aimed Shot.
Always Be Casting
ALWAYS BE CASTING
The problem is not so much that people forget that  Steady Shot exists, however. It is understanding the value of time in the DPS equation. Most people press their buttons rather slowly, they press buttons that are not available to them, their DPS rotation stops when they are focused on a mechanic, or they just have bad reaction times. All of these things lead to lost time where you are not casting anything, often for very small windows of time, but these small windows of time add up very quickly, and they are the difference between a good and a great player. Always be casting something, even if it is just  Steady Shot, and you will see a noticeable increase in your DPS.
